Beat

What she covers, and what travels

Her September work points to a specific set of interests. This section reads those headlines and names the angles that fit, with the caveat that full article text sits behind a paywall.

Independent oversight of AI labs, and whether the watchdogs are genuinely independent. Safety-incident disclosure and reporting frameworks. Researchers who leave labs over safety concerns. Cross-lab model testing. Agentic systems that misbehave, including the swarms that crossed into software they did not own.

She rewards accountability stories with named actors and concrete incidents: a lab, a date, a mechanism, a person. A platform that submits to external verification is a story. A platform that simply asserts its own safety is not. The open question worth putting to her honestly is what a third party could actually verify about a system deployed inside a customer's own cloud.

Her headlines are concrete and noun-led, naming actors like OpenAI, Google DeepMind and Hugging Face rather than abstractions. She fuses a contrarian frame to a hard fact, as in a demand for watchdogs paired with doubt about their independence. The structure is plain colon-and-clause, without hype adjectives.

Working together

Dos and don'ts

Notes for a first meeting with a reporter whose beat is accountability. Written for our team ahead of the September 28 announcement.

Do

Lead with oversight

  • Lead with the accountability angle. Who watches the AI, and are the watchers independent.
  • Bring named, checkable specifics: incident counts, named labs, dates, people, mechanisms.
  • Offer the independence question honestly, including what a third party could verify about a deployment in a customer's own cloud.
  • Put the enterprise facts in her hands ahead of the meeting, including the in-tenant deployment and the air-gapped option.
  • Respect her employer. The Information is a paid, independent newsroom, and anything embargoed stays embargoed.
Don't

Skip the pitch

  • Keep the framing on added capacity, not replacement of people. A replacement claim turns into a labor story.
  • Keep pricing, contract figures and internal economics away from a reporter.
  • Strip the banned language: em dashes, hype adjectives, and claims of model-agnosticism.
  • Keep her distinct from Tiffany C. Li, the law professor. They are different people.
  • Skip fabricated quotes and article summaries. Her article bodies are paywalled, so only headlines and dates are verified.
